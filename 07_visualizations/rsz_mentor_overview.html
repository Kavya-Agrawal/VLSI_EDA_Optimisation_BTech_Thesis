The 30-second explanation

RSZ is the decision layer between timing and geometry.

It reads a placed design and its timing constraints, repeatedly asks “what is failing?”, tests legal netlist transformations, and writes back a faster—but functionally equivalent—physical implementation.

Inputs
Placed databaseinstances, nets, coordinates
Liberty cellsdelay, slew, capacitance tables
SDC constraintsclocks, required arrival times
Wire parasiticsestimated or routed RC

The RSZ decision engine

Collect worst paths and violating endpoints
Generate candidate physical moves
Estimate delay gain cheaply
Speculatively apply and re-time
Commit improvement—or roll it back
Outputs
Resized cellsstronger or weaker drive
Buffered netsnew repeaters and load splits
VT / pin swapsfaster equivalent choices
Legal placementsite-aligned, zero overlap

Algorithm atlas

Six places where a heuristic can change the outcome.

The thesis framework divides the codebase by the decision being made—not merely by directory. Each cluster is a distinct research surface for improving WNS and TNS.

01

Setup strategy

Chooses which endpoints and paths to attack, in what order, with which move sequence and stopping rule.

Research question: how should WNS and TNS effort be budgeted?
02

Cell sizing

Selects a drive strength, VT variant, or equivalent input-pin assignment for a critical driver.

Research question: which discrete cell choice gives the best true path gain?
03

Buffering

Builds or selects buffer trees, splits loads, clones drivers, and removes counterproductive buffers.

Research question: where should topology change, and which loads belong together?
04

Hold repair

Adds controlled data delay while protecting setup timing and avoiding unnecessary area growth.

Research question: how can delay be inserted with minimum setup damage?
05

DRV repair

Repairs excessive slew, capacitance, fanout, or wire length before and during timing closure.

Research question: which electrical violation is the true timing bottleneck?
06

Timing model

Predicts delay and slew gains cheaply enough to rank many moves before invoking precise STA.

Research question: which approximation best predicts the eventual committed gain?

Where the thesis fits

The framework evolves decisions, then demands physical evidence.

Graphify supplies the structural map. The evolution loop uses that map to scope one hypothesis, edit the relevant RSZ cluster, rebuild OpenROAD, and measure post-repair WNS/TNS on placed benchmarks.

The key distinction: an idea is not a result. Only a deterministic candidate that passes build, unit, smoke, placement, hold, DRV, and area guards can become the next champion.
