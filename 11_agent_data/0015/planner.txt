# Iteration 15 plan: Criticality-partitioned load isolation on shared bottleneck nets, as compound moves in the post-stock recovery phase

## Observation
- **Champion (iter 13)** runs the stock LEGACY loop unchanged, then a journaled TNS-recovery phase. Each recovery trial is accepted only if it passes a fixed stock-WNS floor and STA measures a TNS gain. It improved WNS on all four designs and cut Ibex TNS by 32%.
- **What still limits timing is the net structure, not cell sizes:**
  - Ibex's ten worst paths all go through the fanout-943 net driven by `AND2_X1 _26153_/ZN`. Raw slew there is about 2.09e9.
  - JPEG's worst endpoints share the `ddin[8]/Q` launch net (DFFR_X2, fanout 12–17), which feeds HA/FA chains.
  - Iter 14 used sizing-only DP. It did not change either structure, and JPEG rejected 65% of its trials on WNS.
- **Shared-cone repair helps Ibex, but only safely outside the stock loop.** Iter 8 cut Ibex TNS by 57.6 ns, iter 9 by 34.3 ns and iter 13 by 21.3 ns. Iters 8, 9 and 12 changed the stock loop and lost WNS on AES, gcd and JPEG. Iter 13 avoided that by working only after the stock loop.
- **Earlier buffering attempts failed for a specific reason.** Iters 6, 7 and 11 changed generators inside the stock loop and chose partitions by prediction only. Iter 7 lost 5.3 ps of AES WNS from unmeasured collateral damage.
- **The fixed floor lets recovery give back WNS it already gained.** In iter 14 this cost 5.2 ps on Ibex and 1.3 ps on JPEG compared with the champion.

## Cluster choice and why
**buffering.**
- Rotation: iters 12 and 13 were setup_strategy, iter 14 was sizing, so buffering is allowed.
- The evaluator's dominant cause is `high_fanout`, and the stated remedy is to repair the shared structure explicitly. Sizing and ranking changes cannot change the topology of a net, so this needs the buffering cluster.
- I keep the champion's recovery phase as the host and introduce the new move only there. This follows the iter-14 rulebase rule: new generators start inside the post-stock phase.

## Hypothesis
**Problem.** Many violating endpoints share one driver net. Every critical sink on that net pays the delay of the whole load, including sinks that have plenty of slack.

**Current heuristic.**
- `SplitLoadGenerator::generate` splits a net by sink-slack rank, roughly in half. It runs inside the greedy per-endpoint loop, its effect is predicted rather than measured, and it never considers how many violating endpoints sit below each sink.
- `CloneGenerator` duplicates a gate under a similar fixed recipe.
- Champion recovery only tries single moves on shared drivers: three attempts per driver on the top 50.

**New approach: TNS-weighted critical-sink isolation with a measured cut-point search.** This is a compound move in `runTnsRecovery`, applied to the top-K shared bottleneck drivers (ranked by number of violating endpoints in the fan-out cone × summed negative slack).
1. **Score each sink pin** by the violation it carries downstream: the sum of negative endpoint slack reachable through it, taken from the endpoint-to-driver attribution the recovery phase already builds. Sort sinks by this weight. Do not use local slack.
2. **Generate cut points.** Use k ∈ {critical count, 2×, ¼ of fanout, ½ of fanout}, deduplicated and capped at 4. For each k, the top-k sinks stay on the original driver and the rest move behind a new buffer. The buffer is placed at the centroid of the moved sinks and sized from the existing buffer list by their capacitance. For fanout over 64, also try a clone variant: the clone drives the non-critical set.
3. **Optionally resize the driver** for its now-reduced load, inside the same journal.
4. **Evaluate each variant with real incremental STA** under the journal, then commit the best one. A variant must pass two checks:
   - (a) a **dual WNS floor**: WNS ≥ max(stock-final WNS, WNS at the last recovery commit);
   - (b) a strict design-TNS gain.

   The dual floor is a guard, not the paradigm. It answers the iter-14 failure where recovery gave back WNS it had already gained.
5. **Use a separate budget.** This step runs before the champion's existing single-move recovery and has its own budget, so the champion's recovery moves are not crowded out. In iter 14 the DP consumed the shared budget.

**Why WNS and TNS should improve.**
- On Ibex, isolating about 900 non-critical sinks behind one buffer removes most of the load capacitance from `_26153_/ZN`. That should cut its stage delay (a large share of the delay on 39-stage paths) for every violating `rf_reg[*]` endpoint at once, which is the iter-8-style TNS gain without touching the stock loop.
- On JPEG, splitting the `ddin[8]` launch net speeds up the shared start of the HA/FA paths. The dual floor means WNS can only stay equal or improve compared with the champion.

**Risks.**
- The cost and delay of the new buffer may cancel the gain on low-fanout nets such as gcd. Mitigation: only drivers with fanout ≥ 8 and at least 2 violating endpoints in their cone are eligible.
- Legalization may move cells so the measured gain does not survive. Mitigation: insert only at the centroid with legal-site snapping and report final timing after `detailed_placement`.
- Runtime: at most 4 variants × K=40 drivers, each one incremental STA call.

**Path-report signature.**
- Ibex top paths should show a new buffer stage or a reduced load at `_26153_/ZN`, slew there falling by more than 5×, and `rf_reg[703]` slack better than −73 ps.
- JPEG's `ddin[8]/Q` fanout should drop.

## Trigger estimate
- **Ibex:** the stock run makes 172 moves. I expect about 40 eligible drivers × up to 4 STA-evaluated variants (≈160 trials) and 15–30 committed topology changes. That is 9–17% of stock moves, and it changes the load on nets used by hundreds of violating paths.
- **AES:** the stock run makes about 2,078 moves, including 481 buffers. I expect about 40 drivers × 4 ≈ 160 trials and around 20–40 commits. Measured against the 481 buffer moves (the relevant category), that is 4–8%. I'll raise K to 60 on designs with more than 1,000 endpoints to stay above 5%.
- **JPEG:** I expect a similar number of trials.
- Counters will verify these figures.

## Implementation sketch
- **`src/policy/SetupLegacyPolicy.cc` / `.hh`:**
  - Add `runLoadIsolationRecovery()`, called right after the stock loop and before the existing `runTnsRecovery` body.
  - Add `sinkViolationWeights(driverPin)`, which reuses the recovery phase's endpoint-to-driver attribution.
  - Add `isolateLoads(driver, k, mode)`, which builds the move through the existing buffer-insert and clone primitives under a nested journal.
  - Add the dual-floor accept check.
- **`src/move/SplitLoadGenerator.cc`:** add a helper entry point that takes an explicit sink partition and buffer location. The stock `generate()` path stays exactly as it is.
- **`src/move/CloneGenerator.cc`:** add the same kind of explicit-partition helper. Stock behaviour is unchanged.
- **Counters:** print `RSZ_EVOLVE load_iso` lines per design with:
  - drivers examined, variants evaluated, committed, rejected for WNS, rejected for TNS;
  - the chosen k divided by fanout;
  - the predicted versus measured TNS change;
  - the stock-phase move counts, to prove the stock trajectory is byte-identical.

## Success criteria
- Score vs champion ≥ +0.002.
- Ibex TNS better than −40 ns, with WNS no worse than the champion's −74.2 ps.
- JPEG WNS ≥ −85.3 ps and TNS < −31.15 ns.
- AES and gcd WNS unchanged or better.
- At least 10 committed isolations on Ibex, including one on `_26153_`.
- JPEG finishes within the 10-minute cap.

## Fallback if it fails
- If isolations are committed but lost after legalization, change cluster to `timing_model`: add placement-aware estimates for where the buffer can go.
- If nothing reaches commit (for example, only rejections for WNS), the isolation paradigm is falsified for these designs. Then return to setup_strategy with beam search over recovery-move sequences (depth 2), rather than a narrower split variant.
