I replaced the greedy TNS recovery with a depth-2 beam search in `src/policy/SetupLegacyPolicy.cc`. Nothing has been compiled or run, since the task says the orchestrator builds and tests. I did not check the OpenSTA calls the new code relies on (`Path::isClock`, `Path::arrival`) against the build.

The beam search is a new `runBeamRecovery()`, which `runTnsRecovery()` now calls. The old greedy body is renamed `runGreedyTnsRecovery()` and no longer called; it is left in the file, so delete it if you want it gone. `HYPOTHESIS.md` is written in the tree root.

- **Seed ranking:** seeds are ranked by slack-weighted endpoint coverage times arc delay, over data arcs only. Clock-network paths are skipped.
- **Trials:** each of the top 8 seeds gets two single-move variants (best move type, then the next type). Each variant is also paired with its upstream and its downstream driver. Every trial runs in a nested journal and is rolled back.
- **Commit:** the best-TNS sequence of each round is replayed and committed through the journal. A replay that no longer passes is rolled back and ends the search.
- **Acceptance:** WNS must stay at or above a floor that ratchets up after each commit, and TNS must gain at least 0.5 ps. No endpoint at or above -1 ps may end below -1 ps.
- **Limits:** up to 12 rounds, bounded by the existing commit cap of `max(30, stock_moves/10)`.
- **Counters:** `beam_*` counters, including `beam_committed_pair` and `beam_rejected_guard`, are printed in the `RSZ_EVOLVE <name> fired=<n>` format at the end.

The main risk is JPEG runtime: about 8 seeds × 2 variants × 3 trials per round, each needing incremental timing updates. The guards may also be strict enough to leave few commits.
