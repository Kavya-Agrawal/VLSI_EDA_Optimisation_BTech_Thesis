## Observation

Stock repair leaves stressed WNS/TNS of **−0.1083/−14.773 ns on AES**, **−0.2994/−416.273 ns on Ibex**, and **−0.1742/−7.626 ns on gcd**. AES’s worst paths include slow-slew AOI221_X1 and XOR2_X1 drivers; Ibex also has slow data logic, although its fanout-943 clock gate does not establish a data-sizing opportunity. gcd’s limited improvement and an already stronger AOI21_X4 driver suggest a smaller opportunity.

**Iteration 4 supports investigating sizing feasibility:** its feedback identifies a winner-only max-capacitance check that can suppress sizing entirely. It provides no benchmark timing evidence. **Iteration 3 establishes the API-compatibility failure to avoid**, while iteration 4 additionally rules out repeating unconditional absolute-gain ranking with the known failing expectations. Neither experiment establishes which designs improve or regress; iterations 1–2 are infrastructure/no-code records.

## Cluster choice and why

**Choose `sizing`: select the best electrically feasible replacement under the champion’s existing cost-normalized objective.**

This changes the search decision from “choose a winner, then possibly abandon sizing” to “choose the best feasible winner.” It directly addresses a source-confirmed limitation without repeating the failed ranking objective. Whether this limitation materially affects the benchmarks remains unmeasured.

## Hypothesis

**Problem →** A profitable replacement may be suppressed because the highest-scoring master increases capacitance too much on an upstream net, even when another profitable master fits the available budget.

**Current heuristic →** `SizeUpGenerator::upsizeCell()` maximizes positive stage-delay gain divided by the existing cost ratio. `generate()` then calls `replacementPreservesMaxCap()` only on that winner and returns no candidate if it fails. That check examines the replacement’s input-capacitance effects across fanin nets, including inputs outside the selected path.

**New approach →** Keep the champion’s score, upstream loading penalty, transition eligibility, and single-candidate behavior, but select the highest-scoring replacement that passes the existing instance-level max-capacitance check.

**Why WNS/TNS improves →** Previously abandoned targets can receive a smaller, feasible timing improvement. On affected AES or Ibex data paths, reports should show a replacement master, reduced stage delay or slew, and lower matched-endpoint arrival. Logic depth should remain unchanged.

**Risks →** Feasible runners-up may be rare or absent. Local estimated gain may disappear after timing propagation or legalization; additional accepted sizing may consume area or hold margin.

## Implementation sketch

Paths below are relative to the candidate `rsz/` tree.

- **`src/move/SizeUpGenerator.cc` — `generate()`, `selectReplacement()`, `upsizeCell()`**
  - Start from the original champion.
  - Share the existing scoring loop through a file-local selector accepting an optional feasibility predicate. Keep the protected `upsizeCell()` signature and behavior intact.
  - Have `generate()` supply a predicate calling `resizer_.replacementPreservesMaxCap(inst, candidate, active_mode)`.
  - Select the greatest existing score among eligible, feasible masters; preserve iteration order and strict-score tie behavior. Emit at most one candidate and retain the final safety check.
  - Trace rejected unconstrained winners, available feasible runners-up, their scores, and eventual move outcomes. Do not approximate feasibility using only the active input pin.
- **`test/cpp/TestResizer.cc`**
  - Add coverage where the score winner violates an upstream cap limit but a lower-scoring profitable replacement passes.
  - Cover all-infeasible and winner-already-feasible cases, including rejection caused by a non-path input.
  - Preserve the existing skewed-transition master assertions and noncritical-edge rejection coverage.

No changes to `SizeUpGenerator.hh`, `SizeUpCandidate.cc`, or `Resizer.cc` are needed.

## Success criteria

1. All C++ gates and **10/10 smoke tests** pass, including the unchanged transition-selection tests.
2. Benchmark tracing finds **at least one suppressed winner with a feasible profitable runner-up**. Zero occurrences falsifies applicability to this tier.
3. Target **AES WNS improvement ≥0.005 ns and TNS magnitude reduction ≥1%**, with Ibex WNS/TNS nonregressing. Limit gcd WNS loss to **0.001 ns**, with no TNS regression.
4. Achieve mean evaluator score **≥+0.00200**, with all hold, DRV, area, placement, and determinism gates passing.
5. For affected matched endpoints, demonstrate lower data arrival attributable to recovered sizing; compare the five worst endpoints per design after legalization and parasitic re-estimation. Treat summary slew values as raw report units until verified.
