## Hypothesis (problem -> current heuristic -> new approach -> why WNS/TNS improves -> risks)

**Problem:** Cost normalization may favor a smaller timing improvement on a violating path even when another eligible replacement offers greater delay reduction.

**Current heuristic:** `SizeUpGenerator::upsizeCell` estimates stage delay as gate delay plus preceding-driver resistance times input capacitance. Among eligible replacements, it maximizes delay gain divided by a cost ratio: leakage when available, otherwise LEF or Liberty area.

**New approach:** Select the single eligible replacement with the greatest positive, finite estimated stage delay gain. Retain the upstream capacitance penalty, both-edge drive-resistance checks, configured replacement restrictions, and existing max-cap check. Break equal-gain ties deterministically by lower cost, then library and cell name.

**Why WNS/TNS improves:** Greater estimated delay reduction should advance critical data arrivals. On shared data cones, that improvement can benefit several violating endpoints. AES is the primary opportunity; stronger replacements are predicted only where the measured ranking actually differs.

**Risks:** The upstream resistance approximation can underestimate slew and side-path damage. Larger replacements can increase area and placement pressure, or worsen hold timing. Pure gain ranking may also offer little additional headroom in gcd.
