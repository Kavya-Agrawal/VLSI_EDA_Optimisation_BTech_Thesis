# Iteration 10 plan: cluster `sizing`

## Observation
- **Shared cones help Ibex, but the side effects hurt the other designs.** In iterations 8 and 9 (both `setup_strategy`), repairing shared cones cut Ibex TNS a lot: by 57.6 ns in iteration 8 and by 34.3 ns (−52%) in iteration 9. The same changes made AES worse every time: TNS rose by 0.548 ns and then 0.675 ns, and WNS fell by 14–17 ps. In iteration 9 JPEG also lost 3.7 ns TNS, and its resizes fell from 282 to 54. Iteration 9's AES regressions (`sa23_sr[*]`, `sa31_sr[3]`) sit next to a shared local driver, `_13069_/Z`. Better target order did not stop accepted moves from damaging paths beside or upstream of the one being fixed.
- **The remaining JPEG worst paths are deep arithmetic logic.** They have 18–23 stages and weak `HA_X1`, `AOI21_X1` and `OAI21_X1` cells on them, often feeding a shared `BUF_X4`. Each upsize adds input capacitance to an upstream driver that is itself on other violating paths. The stock estimate scores the upstream penalty only by stage delay on the path being repaired. It does not consider how critical that upstream net or the other loads are.
- **Resizing is the move that matters most.** Stock resize counts are: AES 1388 of about 2078 moves, Ibex 149 of 172, JPEG 282 of 399, GCD 24 of 25.

## Cluster choice and why
I am choosing **`sizing`**.
- `setup_strategy` was used in iterations 8 and 9, so the rotation rule rules it out for a third time in a row.
- Buffering made AES worse twice (iterations 6 and 7).
- Earlier sizing attempts changed the wrong thing:
  - Iterations 3 and 4 replaced the ranking metric outright and broke the unit tests' choice of cell (dead ends 3 and 4).
  - Iteration 5 added a feasibility fallback that never triggered (dead end 5).
- None of them changed how a cell size is valued across the paths it affects. That is what the evidence from iterations 8 and 9 points to: the damage comes from paths that share cells with the repaired one, not from which target is picked.

## Hypothesis
- **Problem:** Upsizing one driver for one path slows its upstream driver and pushes extra load onto paths that share it. On AES and JPEG these collateral slowdowns cancel the gains, so WNS gets worse after repair.
- **Current heuristic:** `SizeUpGenerator` (with the estimate in `DelayEstimator`) picks a replacement cell using one stage only: `ΔD = (R_orig−R_new)·C_load − R_prev·ΔC_in`, measured along the one path being repaired. Every arc is weighted the same, whether or not it is critical.
- **New approach: criticality-weighted sizing.** This borrows the Lagrangian-relaxation idea used in `LRSubproblem` and applies it inside greedy sizing. Each candidate cell is scored as the sum of weighted delay changes over the arcs it affects:
  - the target arc, with weight λ_target;
  - the upstream driver's arc, with weight λ_up, for the extra input capacitance;
  - each side load on the input net, with its own weight, for the slower input transition.
- **How the weights are set:** The weight λ for an arc is `max(0, −slack_arc)/|WNS|`, using its existing pin slack. Arcs with non-negative slack get weight 0. If nothing nearby is violating, the score reduces to the stock ranking. That keeps the unit-test cell choices identical, which avoids the iteration 4 failure.
- **Why WNS/TNS should improve:**
  - Sizes that rob critical neighbours lose their ranking, which should stop the AES `_13069_`-style regressions.
  - Sizes that help a target whose upstream net is not critical can win, even when a smaller cell is "cheaper" by stock cost. That is the useful kind of resize in the shared Ibex and JPEG cones.
- **Risks:**
  - Pin slacks can go stale between commits.
  - If the penalty is too strong, sizing may stall on JPEG and repeat iteration 9's collapse in resizes.
  - The weighting adds a small cost per candidate for slack lookups.

## Implementation sketch
- **`src/move/SizeUpGenerator.cc`:** Keep the candidate enumeration, filters and function signatures exactly as they are, including the protected `upsizeCell` API. Change only the scoring comparator so that it calls `weightedSizingGain(candidate)`.
- **`src/DelayEstimator.cc` / `.hh`:** Add a helper that returns the extra delay on the upstream driver and on each side load for a given change in input capacitance. It must reuse the existing estimator, not run STA.
- **`src/LRSubproblem.cc`:** Reuse its λ normalisation if it fits. Otherwise compute λ locally from `sta->pinSlack`.
- **Counters:** Print `RSZ_EVOLVE sizing_scored`, `sizing_choice_differs`, `sizing_rejected_by_side_penalty` and `lambda_nonzero_frac` for each design.

## Trigger estimate
I expect the choice of cell to change on at least 15–30% of resizes on AES (over 200 of 1388) and Ibex (over 25 of 149). A side load or upstream net with negative slack is common in the shared cones, so the penalty should apply often. If `sizing_choice_differs` comes in under 5%, I will treat the hypothesis as not exercised.

## Success criteria
- Score at least +0.002 against the current best.
- AES WNS no worse than −0.0258 ns, and `sa23_sr[*]` no longer getting worse.
- JPEG resizes at least 200, with TNS better than −32.4 ns.
- Ibex TNS at least 10% better than −65.7 ns.
- All unit and smoke gates pass.

## Fallback if it fails
If the cell choices change but QoR regresses, the per-arc weighting is the wrong lever. The next step would move to `timing_model`, using `SkewBudget` and `SequentialTimingEstimator` to budget slack across the register-to-register cones. If the choices barely change, sizing is saturated, and later iterations should leave per-cell selection alone.
